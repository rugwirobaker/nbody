nbody — controls

Two builds of one algorithm: a scalar array-of-structures baseline and a SIMD structure-of-arrays kernel. Same force law, same integration order, same ordered-pair n² traversal. Only the memory layout and the instruction width differ.

Each frame owes ten steps of physics. A panel draws only once it has finished them, so every frame carries the same amount of physics and the fps figure reads as throughput rather than as effort. The ns/tick figure is the force calculation alone — the only part of a step where the two builds differ.

Size is mass: a body's radius is proportional to the square root of its mass, exactly as the merge rule computes it, so two discs at the same overlap are equally close to merging whatever they weigh. Bodies are drawn sixteen times life size, since true radii are a fraction of a pixel — the same factor for every body, so relative sizes and distances stay exact.

Colour is temperature — heat per unit mass, where a merge banks the kinetic energy it destroys and the body then cools. Cold dust is pale blue; a body that has just eaten something runs white and then orange, and burns brighter with it. Size is mass, so the two never mean the same thing, and white cores in the crowd are particles overlapping rather than heat.

drag · wheel
Drag to pan, and use the wheel to zoom about the pointer: whatever is under the cursor stays under it, so point at a cluster and scroll in to pick it apart. Double-click restores the opening view. Each panel carries its own camera and the gestures move only the panel they land in — the two runs diverge after the first few merges, so the same screen position is a different part of a different arrangement in each. The range runs from a half-width of 0.04 disk radii to 63, and it is wide because the system is: merging leaks energy, so the survivors keep drifting outward, half of them past seven disk radii after a hundred seconds. Each panel's gauge reads out where its own view sits in that range. To compare the same window in both, shift-double-click the panel you have framed and the other takes its view; recentre in the bar puts both back where they started.
